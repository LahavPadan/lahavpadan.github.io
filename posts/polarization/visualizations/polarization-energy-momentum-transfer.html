field → energy → mechanical transfer

Why polarized light pushes—and when it twists

Polarization determines how the transverse electric field \(\mathbf E\) moves. Maxwell's equations then determine the energy flux \(\mathbf S\); over a beam area \(A\) and a time \(\Delta t\), that flux delivers the energy \(U=P\Delta t\). The arriving energy always carries forward momentum, while a definite circular rotation also carries spin angular momentum. Absorption transfers those two conserved quantities as force and torque.

the field fixes power per area \(\displaystyle \mathbf B=\frac1c\hat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf E \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf S=\frac1{\mu_0}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B\) Maxwell fixes \(\mathbf B\) once \(\mathbf E\) and the travel direction \(\hat{\mathbf k}\) are known. Hence \(\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\varepsilon_0c\langle|\mathbf E|^2\rangle\hat{\mathbf k}\), where \(|\langle\mathbf S\rangle|\) is average power per unit area.
choose the illuminated area \(\displaystyle P=A|\langle\mathbf S\rangle|\) Multiplying by the beam area \(A\) gives total power.
follow one time interval \(\displaystyle U=P\,\Delta t\) This is the finite energy that reaches the absorber during \(\Delta t\).
