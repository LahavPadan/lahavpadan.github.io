Exact-Bragg penetration

One decay scale, two boundary conditions

At exact Bragg, the bounded semi-infinite mode has \(B_\infty=iA_\infty\), hence \(|A_\infty|=|B_\infty|=e^{-\kappa z}\). When \(\kappa L \gg 1\), the entrance region of the finite mirror becomes indistinguishable from that semi-infinite decay. For the finite mirror, \(A(0)=1\) fixes the amplitude of the wave incident from the left, while \(B(L)=0\) says there is no wave incident from the right. These two conditions determine the solution throughout the grating. The value \(A(L)\) is then not an independent boundary condition but the forward amplitude that reaches the far boundary and exits as the transmitted wave; with \(A(0)=1\), the transmission coefficient is \(T=|A(L)|^2\), so \(A(L)=\sqrt{T}\).
